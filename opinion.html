Why we ask

Do you know how much it costs to bring a new pedal to market? Most of the money is spent long before the first one is sold, so building something players don't want is the most expensive mistake a small company can make.

Typical ranges for a small company, in US dollars. Real costs vary by product, by test lab and by how many countries a product is sold in.

Research and development$10,000–$40,000. Circuit design, simulation, firmware and software, mostly paid for in engineering time.
Prototyping$2,000–$8,000. Breadboard parts, three to five rounds of circuit boards, enclosures and test equipment.
Certifications$3,000–$50,000 or more. The low end is a simple pedal sold in the US and Europe; the high end is a wireless product sold worldwide. The full list is below.
Legal$2,000–$10,000. Trademark filings for the names and patent searches to make sure a design is free to use.
First production run$5,000–$15,000. Factories have minimum orders for boards, parts and enclosures, plus packaging and manuals.

Every certification a pedal might need

Not every product needs all of these. Which ones apply depends on what's inside the pedal and where it's sold.

CertificationWhen it's neededTypical cost
FCC Part 15B (US)Any product with a microcontroller or other digital clock, which includes linked pedals$1,500–$5,000
ICES-003 (Canada)Same as FCC Part 15B; usually reuses the same test report$0–$1,000
CE marking, EMC Directive (EU)Any electronic product sold in Europe$2,000–$6,000
UKCA (UK)Products sold in Great Britain; mostly paperwork on top of CE$0–$1,500
RoHS (EU and others)Electronics sold in Europe: proof the parts are lead-free and free of other banned substances$500–$2,000
REACH (EU)Declarations about chemicals in the materials, collected from suppliers$0–$1,000
WEEE (EU)Registration and recycling fees in each European country where a product is soldHundreds to thousands per country, every year
Low Voltage Directive (EU)Products running on 50 V AC or more. Not a 9 V pedal, but it would apply to a power supply$2,000–$8,000
UL or ETL safety listing (US)Gear that plugs into the wall. Usually avoided by shipping an already listed power adapter$5,000–$20,000
FCC Part 15C, FCC ID (US)Anything with Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or another radio$8,000–$25,000, or $2,000–$6,000 with a pre-certified radio module
Radio Equipment Directive (EU)Wireless products sold in Europe$5,000–$15,000
ISED radio certification (Canada)Wireless products sold in Canada$2,000–$5,000
Bluetooth SIG qualificationAny product that uses Bluetooth or its name and logoAbout $10,000 per product
Wi-Fi Alliance certificationOptional; needed only to use the Wi-Fi CERTIFIED logo$20,000 or more, including membership
USB-IF vendor ID and logoOptional for USB products; needed only for our own USB ID or the USB logo$6,000 for the ID, plus testing for the logo
Other countries (Japan VCCI and MIC, Australia RCM, Korea KC)Selling outside North America and Europe; each country has its own rules$1,000–$10,000 per country

A few minutes of your time tells us where that money is best spent.
